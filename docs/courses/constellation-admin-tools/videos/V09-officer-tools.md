## Purpose

Officers inherit these tools mid-semester with no handover. This video is the handover. Its real
subject is not which buttons exist — it's the judgement attached to two of them.

## Narration

**[00:00 — admin view]**

If you're watching this, you're an officer, and you've probably been handed these tools with no
explanation. Here's the explanation.

**[00:18 — pending rewards]**

This is the queue that matters most. Task completions and any time log over two hours land here
before they pay out.

*(pause)*

Which makes you the reason the XP number means anything. A ledger nobody checks is a ledger nobody
believes, and the whole point of tracking contribution is that it's credible when a member puts it on
an application.

**[00:52 — approve/reject]**

Approve, or reject with a reason.

*(beat)*

Be honest about the standard here. You're not auditing anyone. You're checking that four hours of
work looks like four hours of work. Most of these are ten seconds and yes.

*(pause)*

When something is wrong it's almost always a mistake — someone logged to the wrong task, or logged a
whole week in one entry. Reject with a reason and they'll fix it. Rejecting silently is how you make
someone stop logging time at all.

**[01:26 — reward config]**

You can change what things are worth. Every event type, every amount.

*(pause)*

Do this rarely, and do it out loud. Members plan around these numbers, and quietly halving the value
of logging time reads as a punishment even when it was a spreadsheet decision. Announce it, then
change it.

**[01:56 — event config]**

Event types, RSVP behaviour, reminder timings. Mostly set once a year and left alone.

**[02:20 — member admin]**

Roles, project membership, and the GitHub links that connect a commit to a person.

*(beat)*

Role is what actually governs permissions in Constellation — not rank. Rank is recognition. Role is
access.

**[02:42 — admin view]**

Two things to take away. Clear the reward queue often. And change the numbers rarely, and in public.

*(hold, fade)*

## Notes for the recorder

- Use a **seeded admin account on a demo database**. Do not record the real admin view — the pending
  queue names real members and their hours.
- The rejection guidance is the most important 30 seconds in this course. An officer who rejects
  without a reason permanently suppresses that member's time logging, and the club loses the data.
- "Rank is recognition. Role is access." — the sentence that stops officers from treating XP as a
  permission system.
