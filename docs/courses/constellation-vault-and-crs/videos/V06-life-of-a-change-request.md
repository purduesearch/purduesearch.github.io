## Purpose

A change request is the only part of Constellation that deliberately slows someone down. This video
has to make that trade legible, or CRs get filed as theatre and approved unread.

## Narration

**[00:00 — BOM with one part highlighted]**

Here's a bracket. And here are the four assemblies that reference it, the fixture that was machined
around it, and the order that's already been placed.

*(pause)*

Changing that bracket is not a local decision. That's the whole reason change requests exist.

**[00:24 — new CR]**

A change request says: here's what I want to change, here's why, and here's what it touches.

*(beat)*

The rationale field is not paperwork. In six months, when somebody asks why this part has a notch in
it, this is the only place that answer still exists.

**[00:50 — affected items]**

Constellation works out what's affected from the BOM. You don't have to remember which assemblies use
the part — that's exactly the kind of thing humans get wrong under time pressure.

**[01:20 — reviewer's account]**

The reviewer gets notified. On a club timeline that's usually the sub-team lead, and usually within
the day.

**[01:50 — approve/reject with comment]**

They approve or they reject, and either way they leave a reason.

*(pause)*

Rejecting is not a failure state. "Not until after the design review" is a completely reasonable
answer, and having it written down beats having it said once in a meeting.

**[02:20 — rejected CR]**

A rejected request stays. It doesn't vanish.

*(beat)*

Which matters more than it sounds — the second time someone proposes the same change, the record of
why it was turned down the first time is right there.

**[02:48 — approved → new version]**

On approval, the change becomes real: the item gets its new version, the BOM updates, and everyone
holding a downstream part is notified.

*(pause)*

So the honest summary of a CR is: a change that got looked at by one other person before it became
everyone's problem. On a student team where the person who machined the part graduates in May,
that's not bureaucracy. That's the only memory the project has.

**[03:12 — CR list]**

Next, you'll raise one and review one.

*(hold, fade)*

## Notes for the recorder

- Use two demo accounts. Do not show a real member approving or rejecting anything.
- "The only memory the project has" is the argument the whole video is built on — it's the answer to
  a student team's real objection, which is that they'll all remember. They won't; they graduate.
- Keep the rejected-CR shot. Teams that only ever see approvals learn that rejecting is rude, and
  then reviews stop being reviews.
