> **Hard requirement:** every contact, company, and email address visible in this recording must be
> invented. Sponsor contact details are the most sensitive data in the product and must never appear
> in training material.

## Purpose

Sponsorship and recruiting relationships outlive the members who built them. This video's job is to
make writing things down feel like an obligation to next year's team rather than admin overhead.

## Narration

**[00:00 — contacts list]**

Every company and person the club talks to — sponsors, alumni, faculty, schools we do outreach at —
lives here.

*(pause)*

The reason this exists and a spreadsheet doesn't: the person who built a sponsor relationship
graduates. The relationship shouldn't graduate with them.

**[00:20 — one contact, history]**

Open a contact and you get the history. Who spoke to them, when, what was said, what was promised.

*(beat)*

That last one matters more than it looks. "We said we'd send them photos from competition" is exactly
the sort of thing that gets agreed in a hallway and then quietly doesn't happen for eight months.

**[00:48 — follow-up]**

So a contact can carry a next step: a date, and a person who owns it.

*(pause)*

Not "we should reach out sometime." A date and a name.

**[01:16 — campaigns]**

A campaign is a set of contacts you're working through together — this semester's sponsorship push,
outreach to a district's schools, alumni for the fundraiser.

*(beat)*

It groups the work so you can see coverage: who's been contacted, who's replied, who's been sitting
untouched for three weeks.

**[01:44 — composer]**

Messages are drafted here, and the club's brand voices keep the tone consistent whoever is writing.

*(pause)*

Worth saying plainly: these are assistive. Read what you're about to send. A sponsor can tell when
nobody looked at it, and that costs more than the time it saved.

**[02:12 — Slack reminder]**

Overdue follow-ups come find you. Every weekday morning, a DM listing what's slipped.

*(beat)*

That nudge is the whole difference between a CRM and a graveyard of good intentions.

**[02:38 — contact linked to project]**

And contacts link to projects, so the sponsor who funded the rover is visibly attached to the rover.

Next you'll add a contact and build a small campaign.

*(hold, fade)*

## Notes for the recorder

- Fabricate everything. If in doubt, use obviously fictional companies — a real logo on screen is a
  real problem.
- "A graveyard of good intentions" is the line that justifies the follow-up field. Keep it.
- The "read what you're about to send" caution is not optional. A club that mails AI-drafted text to
  sponsors unread will eventually mail something embarrassing.
