## What you can now do

You can find your way around, read a board and trust it, create and finish a real task, say why
something is stuck in a way the system understands, read a milestone's health, and set Constellation
up to reach you instead of waiting for you.

That's everything a member needs to work here. Genuinely — there is no secret second layer.

## Your training project

The private project you practised in is archived when you pass the final. Nothing in it was ever
visible to anyone else, and none of it earned real XP.

If you want a scratch space again later, ask an officer — there's no cost to making one.

## Things that are true and worth remembering

> **The board is the answer.** When someone asks whether something is done, check the column and
> answer from that. Not from memory, and not from the last thing you heard.

> **Say when you're stuck, in the system.** A blocker takes ten seconds and saves someone else a day.
> Silence is the expensive option.

> **Log your hours.** It's how the club knows what things actually cost, how sponsorship reports get
> written honestly, and how your work is visible when it matters to you.

> **Write titles that end.** "Route and secure the main wiring harness through bay two," not "wiring
> harness." If you can't tell whether it's finished, it isn't a task yet.

## If something breaks or confuses you

- Ask in your project's Slack channel — someone has hit it before.
- If a walkthrough in this course pointed at something that wasn't there, that's a bug in the course
  and it's already been reported automatically. Mention it anyway; it helps to know it mattered.
- If Constellation is wrong about something, say so. An inaccurate board is worth fixing and nobody
  minds being told.

## One last thing

Constellation only works if people put real information into it. A board that says everything is on
track when it isn't is worse than no board — it launders a problem into a green bar.

So: be honest in it. Mark things blocked when they're blocked. Move dates when they've moved. That's
the whole ask.
