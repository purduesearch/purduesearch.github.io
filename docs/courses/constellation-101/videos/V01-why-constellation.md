## Purpose

Answer the question a new member is actually asking, which is not "what does this button do" but
"why am I being handed a fifth piece of software." Everything else in the course assumes the learner
has accepted that Constellation is worth learning. This video is where that happens.

## Narration

**[00:00 — Slack, scrolling a thread]**

Here's how a club project usually dies. Someone asks who's doing the wiring harness. Three people
answer. Two of them are answering a different question. Somebody says "I'll take it" on a Tuesday,
and by the following Tuesday nobody can find the message.

*(pause — let the scroll land)*

Nothing here is anyone's fault. Chat is built to move forward. It is not built to tell you the state
of anything.

**[00:18 — fade to Constellation dashboard]**

This is Constellation. It's SEARCH's project system, and it exists for exactly one reason: to hold
the state of the club's work somewhere that doesn't scroll away.

**[00:40 — pan across the board]**

Every piece of work is a task. Every task sits in one of four columns, and that column is the truth
about it — not what someone said in chat, not what you remember agreeing to. To-do, in progress,
blocked, done.

*(pause)*

If you want to know what's happening on a project, you look at this. That's the whole idea.

**[01:05 — open a task card]**

And a task isn't just a title. Who's on it. When it's due. What "finished" actually means. The
conversation about it. The hours logged against it. All of it in one place, attached to the work
instead of scattered across a channel.

**[01:30 — Slack DM arriving beside the board]**

Now — Constellation does not replace Slack. Slack is still where you talk. What changes is that
Constellation tells Slack when something needs you. You get a direct message when you're assigned
something, when you're mentioned, when something you're waiting on comes unblocked.

*(pause)*

You don't have to live in Constellation. It comes and finds you.

**[01:52 — profile, rank bar, XP history]**

The last piece is this. Constellation keeps score. You earn XP for finishing work and logging hours,
you move up the rank ladder, you pick up doubloons you can actually spend.

*(beat)*

That's not there to gamify you into working weekends. It's there because club work is volunteer work,
and volunteer work that nobody notices is work that stops happening. This is the system noticing.

**[02:14 — dashboard, wide, hold]**

So: one place for the state of the work, and it comes to you when you're needed.

The rest of this course is you actually driving it. Next up, we'll walk the interface together.

*(hold two seconds, fade)*

## Notes for the recorder

- The Slack thread in the opening shot must be **fabricated**. Do not screen-capture a real channel —
  real names and real disagreements do not belong in onboarding material.
- The line "not there to gamify you into working weekends" is the one line in this course that
  addresses the obvious objection to the reward system. Don't cut it for time; cut a shot instead.
- If the dashboard looks empty on your capture account, seed it first. A tour of an empty product is
  an advertisement for not using the product.
