## Purpose

Close the loop the course opened in V01. A member who thinks Constellation is a website they have to
remember to visit will stop visiting it. This video's job is to establish that it reaches out.

## Narration

**[00:00 — notification bell]**

Constellation has its own notification centre, and it's the complete record — every mention, every
assignment, everything you're waiting on.

*(pause)*

But almost nobody's first move in the morning is opening a project tool. So it doesn't rely on that.

**[00:18 — split screen, assign → DM]**

Watch. Someone assigns you a task on the left.

*(beat — let the DM land)*

And on the right, you get a Slack DM. Same for an at-mention in a comment, same for a task you were
blocked on coming free, same for a due date coming up.

*(pause)*

That is the actual contract between these two tools. Slack is where you already are. Constellation
knows when to interrupt you there.

**[00:42 — notification preferences]**

And you control it. Every one of these categories is a toggle — in-app, Slack, or neither.

*(beat)*

If it's too noisy, turn things off here rather than muting the whole thing. A member who mutes
Constellation entirely is a member nobody can reach.

**[01:06 — project channel, completion post]**

Some things go to the project channel instead of your DMs. A task hitting done, a milestone landing,
a pull request merging against a linked repo.

*(pause)*

Those are for the group, so they're posted where the group is.

**[01:30 — Monday digest]**

Monday morning you get a digest — what's due this week, what's overdue, what's waiting on you. It's
the one message worth reading before you plan anything.

**[01:54 — calendar and RSVP]**

And meetings live on the calendar here. Events, RSVPs, and scheduling polls — so "when is everyone
free" stops being a forty-message thread.

**[02:14 — dashboard]**

You don't have to remember to check Constellation. Set your preferences once and let it find you.

*(hold, fade)*

## Notes for the recorder

- Both Slack panes must be a **demo workspace**. Do not capture the club's real Slack — a digest
  screenshot leaks real project names and real people's availability.
- The split-screen at 00:18 is the single most persuasive shot in the whole course. Record it
  properly: real assignment, real DM, no cuts between them. If the DM batcher delays it, wait and let
  the take run long, then trim.
- "A member who mutes Constellation entirely is a member nobody can reach" — keep it. It converts a
  settings screen into a reason to care.
