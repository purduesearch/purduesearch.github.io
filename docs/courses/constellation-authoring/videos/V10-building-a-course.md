## Purpose

Hand the course system to the next author. The one thing this video must land is *which section kind
to reach for* — the most common authoring mistake is putting a reference table in a video and a
narrative in a slide deck.

## Narration

**[00:00 — courses list]**

If you're taking this, you're going to write training material for the club. Here's the machine.

**[00:20 — section rail]**

A course is modules, and a module is sections. Modules gate: finish this one to unlock the next. A
section is one piece of teaching, and there are five kinds.

**[00:52 — cycling the kinds]**

Picking the right one is most of the job, so here's the short version.

*(beat)*

**Content** is prose. Use it for the "why" — context somebody reads once.

**Video** is for anything where watching someone do it beats reading about it. Expensive to make and
expensive to change, so use it for things that won't move next semester.

**Slides** is for reference material. If the learner will come back to look something up, a deck
keeps it scannable — a video buries it.

**Quiz** isn't assessment so much as retrieval. People remember what they had to recall.

*(pause)*

And **Walkthrough** is the product driving itself — spotlighting real buttons in the real app.

**[01:26 — module settings]**

Per module you set required, and sequential — whether sections must be done in order or in any order.
Default to sequential. If order doesn't matter, you probably have a reference document rather than a
course.

**[01:52 — quiz builder]**

Every question takes an explanation, shown after grading. Write it. A question that just says "wrong"
is a filter; a question that explains is a second chance to teach.

**[02:18 — AI generation]**

There's a generator. It proposes an outline from a prompt, you edit the outline, and it drafts the
bodies.

*(pause)*

It's genuinely useful for structure and first drafts. Two limits worth knowing. It can't write
walkthrough steps — it has no idea what's on screen. And it will state things about Constellation
confidently and incorrectly, because it's writing from your prompt rather than from the code.

*(beat)*

Generate the skeleton. Verify every factual claim yourself.

**[02:42 — assign]**

Then assign it, with a due date. Members get notified, and you can see who's finished.

*(hold, fade)*

## Notes for the recorder

- The section-kind rundown at 00:52 is the payload of the whole video. Don't rush it, and don't
  reorder it — it runs cheapest-to-make first.
- "Generate the skeleton. Verify every factual claim yourself." is the line that keeps confidently
  wrong training material out of the club. Do not soften it.
- Mention that walkthrough steps are edited as repo files and reviewed like code. An author who
  expects to write tours in the editor should find that out here, not from a greyed-out button.
